# Persona: The Product Visionary

## Overview

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Role** | Founder, small business owner, or product owner with an idea for software |
| **Technical Proficiency** | Low to moderate — comfortable with tools like Teams, Slack, Notion, or basic project management software. Not a developer. |
| **Usage Frequency** | Occasional — 1-3 discovery sessions per quarter when starting new projects or features |
| **Primary Device** | Cross-device; starts discovery on desktop, reviews documents on mobile |

## Primary Tasks

- Articulate a product idea through guided conversation (voice or text)
- Invite collaborators (co-founders, advisors, subject matter experts) to participate
- Review and validate AI-generated discovery documents
- Hand off completed discovery package to developers or AI agents

## Information Needs

- Clear guidance on what information is needed from them
- Structured questions that help translate vision into requirements
- Visual confirmation that their input was captured correctly
- Understanding of what happens next after discovery

## Context

- Works across devices; may start discovery on desktop, review documents on mobile
- Often thinks verbally — prefers talking through ideas over writing them
- May not know what information developers need; relies on the platform to ask the right questions
- Values speed but needs thoroughness

## Pain Points

- Struggles to translate vision into structured requirements
- Has hired developers who "built the wrong thing" due to miscommunication
- Documentation feels tedious; conversations feel natural but don't get captured
- Repeating the same context to multiple developers/vendors

## Current Workarounds

- Taking notes during brainstorming sessions (incomplete capture)
- Recording Zoom calls and manually extracting requirements
- Writing long emails/docs that developers skim
- Multiple iteration cycles to correct misunderstandings

## Success Definition

> "I described my idea in a conversation, and the output was clear enough that developers knew exactly what to build."
